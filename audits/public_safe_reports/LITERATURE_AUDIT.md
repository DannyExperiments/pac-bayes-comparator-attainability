# Q949 literature assessment

Checked 2026-11-27. Confidence is high in the match to the source's comparator and expectation. Confidence is moderate in the bounded finding that no checked work proves this strict common-comparator gap.

- **Exact source.** Foong, Bruinsma, Burt and Turner, *How Tight Can PAC-Bayes be in the Small Data Regime?*, NeurIPS 2021, §5 Open Problem 1, printed and PDF p. 10. It asks whether one convex comparator can attain the expected conjectured PAC-Bayes-kl bound for arbitrary dataset distributions, and asks how close it can get. Theorem 3 includes the supremum of the binomial moment. Equation (6) takes the upper inversion and gives 1 on an empty sublevel set. Equation (7) drops the PAC-Bayes-kl moment penalty to define the benchmark. The latest author version, arXiv v4, January 13, 2022, retains this question. [Final §5](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/214cfbe603b7f9f9bc005d5f53f7a1d3-Paper.pdf#page=10) · [Author v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03542v4#page=10)
- **Exact known bounds and numerical comparison.** Theorem 4 optimizes at fixed $(q,K)$, and Remark 2 makes its pointwise comparison explicit. Corollary 3 gives the expectation lower bound and an equality example with a deterministic sample law. Figure 2(d) numerically compares the best Catoni bound with another comparator. This is a different question from the existence of a shared comparator attaining the lower benchmark. Appendix H works out a setting for comparing the expectation benchmark with Catoni bounds. The present proof uses the shared normalized moment explicitly to force a strict gap for every comparator. [Theorem 4 and Remark 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.03542v4#S3) · [Appendix H](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.03542v4#A8)
- **Later comparator comparisons.** Hellström and Guedj, *Comparing Comparators in Generalization Bounds*, AISTATS 2024, Theorem 4 concerns average quantities. Theorem 8 addresses samplewise bounds. Equation (36) compares the idealized Cramér inversion with moment factor 1 to generic comparator bounds. Equation (37) retains the factor in the valid result. Their discussion explicitly explains the distinction. Neither theorem requires or shows simultaneous equality with the idealization over draws. [Proceedings paper and PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/hellstrom24a.html)
- **Different proof techniques and background.** Kuzborskij et al., *Better-than-KL PAC-Bayes Bounds*, COLT 2024, changes posterior-prior divergence and uses coin-betting; this is outside the designated generic proof family. The source links the removal of conventional PAC-Bayes slack to Langford's 2002 thesis Problem 6.1.2. That broader motivation is separate from common-comparator attainability. The ordinary KL and Catoni comparators used as upper estimates here are classical; sharper ordinary KL binomial moment estimates are noted in source Corollary 2. [COLT paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/kuzborskij24a.html) · [Langford thesis](https://hunch.net/~jl/projects/prediction_bounds/thesis/thesis.pdf) · [Source p. 3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/214cfbe603b7f9f9bc005d5f53f7a1d3-Paper.pdf#page=3)

Searches used the source title and authors, the exact conjectured PAC-Bayes-kl terminology, “attainability”, “expected”, “common comparator”, “convex comparator”, “moment” and “counterexample”.
